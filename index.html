What we are explaining

Revenue across the whole Google Ads account, not one campaign. August came in at $63,103 over 27 days. Scaled to a full month that is about $72,400 against $87,100 last August, a fall of roughly 17%. Over the same window the ad account spent 30% less.

How the next six slides work

Revenue from ads is only ever three things multiplied together: how many clicks you buy, how often they convert, and how much each order is worth. We take them one at a time. Two of the three got better. Only one got worse, and the rest of the deck is about why.

One note on the numbers

Your buyers take weeks to convert, so recent months are still filling in. Every revenue and conversion figure here is projected to full maturity using your own account's lag curve, so this year and last year compare fairly.

None of them are warning-light companies

Not one of your real competitors buys a single Google ad. Whelen, SoundOff, Code 3, Abrams and Federal Signal have no advertiser record at all. The money bidding against you is off-road and truck-accessory brands who found their way into your category.

Why they landed on exactly those two categories

A surface-mount strobe pod and an off-road LED pod are the same object with a different flash pattern, and Google cannot tell them apart. Visor lights, sirens and dash lights are police-only shapes with no consumer equivalent, which is precisely why those stayed flat.

The dates line up with your costs

The arrivals cluster in May to September 2025. That is the window your cost per click started climbing and never came back down. This is a category being entered from next door, not a lighting market that got tougher.

How the bidding works

You tell Google to return 2.35 for every dollar. The most it will pay for a click is that visitor's worth divided by 2.35. If clicks get dearer and the visitor is worth the same, it cannot pay the going rate, so it simply stops entering those auctions.

What happened here

The value of a visitor to this campaign held flat at $2.74 while the price of reaching one rose 29%. Its return fell from 2.25 to 1.75, under the target it was given. So it bought less, month after month, for the better part of a year.

After

The trade

The return fell from 2.36 to 1.85. That is the deal on offer: more volume at a lower return. Where to sit on that line is a business decision and it belongs to you, not to us.

So what can actually be done

OptionVerdictWhat the numbers say
Raise pricesStrongestA 10% rise lifts revenue per click from $3.50 to $3.85, which lifts what the same target will bid from $1.48 to $1.62. That is roughly the whole cost increase, recovered. It is the only lever that fixes the cause rather than trading volume for efficiency. Start with grille and surface mount, where costs rose 74% and where you are already the cheapest certified option. In practice this means trimming a 38% to 64% discount, not raising a sticker price. If it costs more than 10% of conversion rate it is a net loss, so test before rolling out.
Lower the target furtherA trade, not a fixIt buys volume at a worse return, exactly as it did on August 10 when 2.35 to 2.00 doubled spend and took the return from 2.36 to 1.85. Legitimate if you want the revenue, but it does not make anything more profitable.
Lower the budgetDoes nothingThe account has $3,887 a day available and spent $984, a quarter of it. No campaign is capped. Cutting a budget nothing is reaching changes nothing at all.
